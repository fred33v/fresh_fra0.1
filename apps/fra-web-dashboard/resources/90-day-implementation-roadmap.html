90-Day Implementation Roadmap

A practical plan for putting reasonable fraud prevention procedures in place over three months, in line with the six principles of the Home Office guidance on the failure to prevent fraud offence and the Government Functional Standard GovS 013. Adjust the timing to the size and risk of your organisation.

Days 1–30: Mobilise and assess

ActionSuggested ownerDone
Name the board-level person accountable for fraud prevention.Board / Chair
Agree the scope: business areas, subsidiaries, and the agents and contractors who act for you.Accountable person
Complete the fraud risk assessment and record each risk with an owner.Finance lead
Make sure staff, contractors and the public can report concerns, including anonymously.HR / Governance
Start a log of suspected fraud incidents and their outcomes.Counter fraud lead

Days 31–60: Design and implement

ActionSuggested ownerDone
Agree an action plan for every high residual risk, with deadlines.Risk owners
Approve the counter fraud policy and response plan, and the gifts, hospitality and conflicts of interest policies.Board
Tighten core controls: segregation of duties, approval limits, call-back checks on bank detail changes.Finance lead
Introduce proportionate due diligence for new suppliers and other associated persons.Procurement lead
Start fraud awareness training for all staff, beginning with high-risk roles.HR / Training

Days 61–90: Monitor and report

ActionSuggested ownerDone
Run one detection exercise in the highest-risk area, such as data matching on payroll or suppliers.Counter fraud lead
Bring training completion to at least 80% of staff.HR / Training
Review the risk register and progress on each action plan.Accountable person
Report to the board: risks, actions, training, incidents, losses and recoveries.Accountable person
Set the cycle: quarterly register reviews and a full reassessment at least every year.Board

Evidence to keep

Keep dated records as you go. They are what demonstrates reasonable procedures if they are ever tested:

  • Board minutes showing the accountable person, policy approval and oversight
  • The fraud risk assessment and risk register, with owners and action plans
  • Due diligence records for suppliers and associated persons
  • Training records and completion rates
  • Reporting routes, the incident log and how each case was handled
  • Monitoring, detection and board reports

This roadmap is general guidance. It supports, but does not replace, legal advice or a formal GovS 013 assurance review.
